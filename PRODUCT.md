# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Next.js (App Router), TypeScript, Tailwind CSS — specified by user.

## Users

Hiring managers, engineering managers, and tech leads evaluating a senior backend engineer for staff-level or senior roles. Secondary audience: potential clients assessing technical credibility for consulting or contract work.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio that demonstrates systems thinking, architectural judgment, and measurable impact — not visual design skill. Success means a visitor can assess technical depth, relevant stack experience, and proof of delivery within two minutes of scanning.

## Positioning

Backend systems engineer who designs APIs, data layers, and service boundaries in .NET — with enough full-stack fluency (MERN, Next.js) to own integration surfaces without hand-waving.

## Operating Context

Visitors arrive from LinkedIn, résumé links, or referrals. They skim on desktop during work hours. They want case studies with architecture decisions and outcomes, not hero animations or contact-form friction.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Single-page portfolio with: hero, selected work (3–4 case studies), tech stack (grouped by depth), experience timeline, contact links.
- No contact form unless explicitly requested.
- Case study content is illustrative/synthetic until the owner supplies real project details.
- Contact links use placeholder URLs; owner must replace with real profiles.

## Brand Commitments

- Tone: engineering blog meets product page — clarity and information density over decoration.
- Explicit anti-patterns: Inter-on-purple-gradient hero, glassmorphism, nested cards, generic centered hero with glowing metrics, gradient blobs, emoji icons.
- Typography: distinct grotesque or serif headings + monospace for code, tags, metadata.
- Color: near-monochrome OKLCH base, one accent for links/CTAs, no gradients.
- Layout: asymmetric grid, real whitespace.
- Motion: minimal — subtle hovers only.

## Evidence on Hand

No real case studies, metrics, employer names, or contact URLs supplied. All work examples, timelines, and links are synthetic placeholders labeled for replacement.

## Product Principles

1. Prove systems thinking — show problems, decisions, and outcomes, not feature lists.
2. Respect the reader's time — scannable structure, no filler copy.
3. Honest stack depth — group technologies by proficiency, not icon soup.
4. Technical credibility over visual novelty.
5. Every claim must be replaceable with the owner's real data without restructuring the site.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast, keyboard navigation, semantic landmarks, `prefers-reduced-motion` support.
